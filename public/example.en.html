On Reading Slowly

A good sentence is too precious to read once and pass by.
Let’s leave a little room for our thoughts to stay.

3 min read · LibreMemo example document

Every day we open and close many pages. Some sentences stay with us, but once the tab is closed, we often forget where we read them. When we read at the speed of the web, there is less and less room for thoughts of our own.

Saving a page is a small habit that makes that room. Underline the sentences you want to read again, and write a question next to the parts you don’t understand. Taking notes is a way to make reading last a little longer.

Choose the highlighter in the toolbar, then drag across the sentences below. You can also draw with the pen, or leave your thoughts with the note tool. Press the selected highlighter, underline or note button again to choose text/area or note box/free text mode. Annotations are saved automatically, separate from the original, in the app and in your dedicated storage folder. To send your annotations to someone else, use Export at the top to save an Archive with original or a Reading copy (HTML).

1. Reading is paying attention

Reading slowly does not necessarily mean reading at a slow pace. It means asking yourself what matters while you read. Just picking one sentence that stays with you can change the direction of your reading.

Good thoughts grow when you read again.

A word you passed over the first time looks different on a second reading. Looking at yesterday’s note in the same document, you can also see how far today’s thinking has come.

2. Leaving thoughts in the margins

There is no right answer for a note. A short impression, a question that came to mind, or a link to another piece of writing is enough. As the margins of a document fill up little by little with your own words, the saved page becomes your own material.

  • Mark sentences to reread with the highlighter.
  • Set key words apart with an underline.
  • Draw the connections between sentences with the pen and shapes.
  • Write down questions to pick up next time with a note.

3. Open it again anytime

LibreMemo keeps the original document separate from your annotations and bookmarks. Once the app is ready for offline use, you can read saved documents without the internet. To share your annotations, use Export; to move your whole Library, copy the entire dedicated folder. Switch views with the Contents, Search, Annotations and Bookmarks icons in the sidebar, and mark your place with Save current position in Bookmarks. Documents without contents can still use all the other features. If you closed the sidebar, reopen it with ☰ in the document toolbar or with … → Open sidebar in the floating toolbar.

Now go back to the first heading and read the sentences again. Press the Back button at the top to return to where you clicked the link.
